Invoice vs Receipt: What's the Difference?

They both involve money changing hands, but an invoice and a receipt do different jobs — and mixing them up can cause real confusion with clients and accounting.

The short version

An invoice is a request for payment, sent before money changes hands. A receipt is proof that payment was already made, given after. One asks for money; the other confirms it arrived.

InvoiceReceipt
Sent before paymentGiven after payment
Requests a specific amount by a due dateConfirms an amount was received
Includes payment terms and due dateIncludes payment date and method
Used to track what's owedUsed as proof of purchase
Sent by the seller to request paymentSent by the seller to confirm payment

Why the difference matters

If you send a client a "receipt" for work they haven't paid for yet, it's confusing — a receipt implies the transaction is already complete. Likewise, if a client asks for a receipt after paying an invoice, sending them the original invoice again doesn't actually confirm the payment was received on your end.

For accounting and tax purposes, the distinction matters too: invoices show what was billed, receipts show what was actually collected. Businesses often need both records to reconcile their books.

Do you need to send both?

Typically: yes. Send the invoice first to request payment. Once it's paid, a simple confirmation — even just marking it "paid" with a note on the same document — often covers the receipt requirement for smaller transactions. Some clients, especially larger companies, may still ask for a separate receipt for their own records.
